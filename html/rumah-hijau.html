An eco-friendly home

An eco-friendly home is designed to save energy, save water, and stay comfortable to live in. Some of the ideas can be started at low cost.

Ideas for your home

🪟

Cross ventilation

Windows on two sides of a room let air flow through, so the house stays cooler without relying much on fans or AC.

SDG 11
🌳

Shade trees

Plants around the house block the sun's heat and freshen the air.

SDG 13
☀️

Solar panels

The roof can be used to generate electricity from sunlight.

SDG 7
🌧️

Rainwater harvesting

Rainwater can be collected to water plants or wash vehicles.

SDG 12
♻️

Sort your waste

Separate organic and inorganic waste, then turn organic waste into compost.

SDG 12
💡

Efficient lights and appliances

Choose LED lights and appliances with an energy-saving label when buying or replacing.

SDG 7

Data: LED lights can save up to about 90% of electricity compared with older bulbs. Source: Listrik Indonesia, "12 Tips Hemat Penggunaan Listrik Agar Tagihan Tidak Melonjak".

Solar energy for the home

How it works

Rooftop solar panels turn sunlight into electricity that can be used at home.

Why it suits Indonesia

Indonesia is in the tropics with plenty of sunshine all year round, so its potential is large.

Cleaner power

Electricity from the sun reduces the need for power from fossil-fuel plants, which helps cut emissions.

Data: Indonesia's solar energy potential is about 207.8 GWp, and its rooftop solar potential is about 32.5 GW. In April 2026, national rooftop solar capacity reached 1.3 GW. Source: Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (as cited by Kompas and Katadata).

Where to start?

What you can do today

Open the windows during the day, replace broken bulbs with LED, and unplug appliances you are not using.

What you can plan this month

Plant trees or shade plants, and start sorting your waste at home.

What you can plan for the long term

Install solar panels or improve the ventilation design when renovating your home.
